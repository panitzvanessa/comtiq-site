Question this report answersOnce Part 108 is final, where can a commercial operator fly at scale, and which open choices in Parts 108, 146 and 74 decide what that access costs?

Bottom line up front

The two rules are written to depend on each other. Under , a Part 108 flight inside a UAFR follows Part 74, and Part 74 lets it cross a standard UAFR only with and a notice to the site manager before each flight. A special UAFR stays closed to outside operators without the .

The per-flight notice in § 74.255 is the provision most likely to change in practice, because the FAA itself floated a and the delivery companies and drone trade groups that filed in both dockets, joined by Florida Power & Light, say a flight-by-flight notice cannot keep up with . We expect the duty to stay in the rule text. Whether the FAA names an automated way to meet it is a roughly even chance, and either path helps operators less than an exemption would. The open risk sits where the FAA has put no price, as Disney, IAAPA and the electric utility associations ask to remove or condition commercial access to their sites, ask the FAA to say Part 74 does not preempt state laws on drones near critical sites, and the cost analysis states the FAA on what detours cost operators.

Where the two rules meet

The cross-docket findings

The text ties the rules together. Proposed bars Part 108 flights inside a UAFR "unless allowed pursuant to part 74," and the BVLOS preamble promises to if Part 74 comments change the requirement. Part 74 expects delivery now flown under Part 135 to on the BVLOS rule's compliance dates. The Small UAV Coalition called § 108.220 for a rule it expected to finish alongside Part 108. Part 108 reached OIRA on July 10, 2026. Part 74 has not.

Method, sources and limits

How we built this report

Collection. The Rulemaking Observatory pulled every posted record in both dockets through the Regulations.gov API, with detail and text, and we downloaded attachments for every "see attached" record, every organization and every record whose text points to an attachment. We read 1,008 files from 889 BVLOS comments and 271 files from 257 Part 74 comments, using text extraction and OCR. Three of those BVLOS comments yielded no extractable text. Four BVLOS placeholders and one Part 74 placeholder had no downloadable file. One Part 74 record has no receive date and is counted by its posting date.

Coding. Provision tags come from regular-expression lexicons per docket, published with the data. Campaigns are connected groups of five or more comments of at least 200 characters, linked when their word 5-gram TF-IDF vectors meet cosine 0.80. Moving the threshold from 0.75 to 0.90 shifts the BVLOS campaign count from 815 to 745 comments and the Part 74 count from 397 to 330, so the shares in this report sit inside a band of a few points. Single-link grouping can chain unlike texts together, so we checked the mean pairwise similarity inside every cluster. None falls below 0.79.
