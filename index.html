How it works

Every number traces back to a comment you can open.

Each report follows the same seven steps and the same structure, so you know what you will receive. Every finding is checked by an analyst and linked back to the original comment.

  1. Intake

    Your question, your dockets, your deadline.

  2. Collection

    Every comment and attachment, saved as a dated snapshot.

  3. Processing

    Duplicates removed, campaign letters grouped, organizations identified.

  4. Analysis

    AI sorts stance, topic and arguments. An analyst reads every comment behind a key finding.

  5. Quality check

    Every number is checked again against the snapshot before release.

  6. Delivery

    The report and its data arrive in your inbox. Nothing new to install or learn.

  7. Feedback

    A short debrief. The next report shows what changed.

Inside a report

The bottom line first. The evidence right under it.

Every report opens with two or three sentences you can act on. Key findings carry a confidence level, forecasts use fixed likelihood terms, and an appendix links each cited comment in the docket.

At the final rule stage we match each significant argument to the agency's response, and flag the ones it left unanswered or answered thinly. The Administrative Procedure Act requires an answer to significant issues.

Labor Department independent contractor rule

WHD-2026-0001

The proposal to return to the 2021 test for employee or independent contractor status under the FLSA, the FMLA and the MSPA. Four forms wrote 95.9% of the typed comments. The open question is what counts as control in app-based work.

For app platforms, staffing and financial services firms, trucking and construction associations, multi-state employers and worker groups.

BLM-2025-0037

The June 2026 proposal to rewrite the rules for federal onshore oil and gas leasing. Producer groups that back lower bonds ask the BLM to keep a comment period and a longer protest window, and the bond cut carries the litigation risk.

For independent and mid-size producers, sureties, investors in federal leases, producing states, ranch and conservation groups, and energy law firms.

FAA-2025-1908 · FAA-2026-4558

The BVLOS rule, now at White House review, and the proposal that lets critical sites restrict drones overhead. Part 108 decides who may fly beyond sight, and Part 74 decides where they can reach. Six forecasts to score when the final rules publish.
