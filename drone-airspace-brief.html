Part 108 decides who may fly beyond sight, and Part 74 decides where they can reach

FAA-2025-1908 (Parts 108 and 146, final rule at OIRA since July 10, 2026) · FAA-2026-4558 (Part 74, comments closed Aug. 5, 2026)

QuestionOnce Part 108 is final, where can a commercial operator fly at scale, and which open choices in Parts 108, 146 and 74 decide what that access costs?

Bottom line

Under § 108.220, a Part 108 flight inside a UAFR follows Part 74, which lets it cross a standard UAFR only with Remote ID, a fast transit and a notice to the site manager before each flight. Special UAFRs stay closed to outside operators without the approval of the using agency and the FAA. We expect the per-flight notice to stay in the rule, and whether the FAA names an automated way to meet it is a roughly even chance. The unpriced risks sit with facility owners who want commercial flights removed, state laws on drones near critical sites, and detour costs the FAA says it has no data on.

What the record shows

  • 62organizations filed in both dockets. Electric utilities ask for access as drone operators and for control as site owners.
  • 500flights a day at one utility, Florida Power & Light. Delivery firms cite thousands, and say a manual notice per flight cannot keep up.
  • Noneof the cost of operator detours or notices is in the FAA's estimate, which prices facilities and the FAA at $21.4M to $30.6M a year at 7%.
  • 10state attorneys general ask the FAA to leave state drone laws standing. Amazon, CTIA, NRF and the U.S. Chamber ask for express preemption.
  • 5,598posted comments read across both dockets, with 1,279 attachments.

Who should act now

  • Delivery operatorsAsk for an EO 12866 meeting on § 108.220 and the TSA layer while Part 108 sits at OIRA. Check routes against rail terminals, substations, chemical sites, prisons and parks, and against state overflight laws.
  • Utilities, energyPick the role that leads. Facility consent in Part 74 could slow your own inspection contractors. Draft a standing-access list now.
  • Venues, hospitalsExpect no veto for site managers. Plan on detection, Remote ID logging and special UAFRs where a federal partner exists.
  • Manned operatorsPlan EC equipage for low-altitude flying before the final § 108.195 text.
  • UTM providersBuild an automated UAFR notice service that fits the FAA's "form and manner" language.
  • InvestorsPrice delivery networks on routes that clear UAFRs and state laws, not on Part 108 alone.
